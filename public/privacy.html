Final Countdown Timer

Privacy

The Final Countdown Timer has no accounts, no ads and no sign-up. It collects usage statistics through Google Analytics and keeps a public count of visits and shares. Nothing is sold.

Last updated November 13, 2026.

Google Analytics

Every page loads Google Analytics 4 to measure how the site is used. Besides page views, the timer records these interactions: starting, pausing, finishing and resetting a timer (with its duration), switching the music source, sharing a timer (and whether it went through the share sheet or the clipboard), and opening a shared link.

  • In the EU, the UK and Switzerland, analytics cookies are off by default. Google receives these events without cookies or identifiers, so visitors there are not recognized from one visit to the next.
  • Everywhere else, Google Analytics sets its first-party cookies to tell new visitors from returning ones.
  • Advertising features are off everywhere. Nothing is used for ads or ad personalization.
  • Event data is kept for 14 months.

Google processes this data under its own terms: how Google uses information from sites that use its services. Browser extensions that block analytics work normally here, and the timer works the same with them on.

The visit and share counts

The numbers under the title and under the Share button are running totals. Each load of the timer adds one visit, and each share adds one share. Only the two totals are stored: no IP address, cookie, device identifier or timestamp is kept with them.

Music

  • YouTube, the default source, loads YouTube's player script and embeds the video in YouTube's privacy-enhanced mode (youtube-nocookie.com). Loading the player sends a request to YouTube, and YouTube's own privacy policy applies to what it collects.
  • Synth is generated in your browser. It makes no network requests.
  • Your own file plays locally. It is never uploaded, and it is gone when you close the page.

Hosting

The site is served by Cloudflare, which processes each request, including your IP address, in order to deliver the page. The timer itself stores nothing in your browser: no local storage and no cookies of its own.

Questions

The site's source code is public, so you can check all of this yourself. Questions or requests can go to the project's issue tracker.
